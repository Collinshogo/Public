Before you start

Get these three things ready

The bot is a strategy that runs on your TradingView chart. A service called CrossTrade carries its orders to your Tradovate account. Every account is your own, and All Fluence Trading never has access to any of them.

Do the setup on a computer, in a web browser. TradingView, CrossTrade and Tradovate all open as websites. You can read along on your phone.

Words used in this guide
  • Webhook: a message one website sends to another by itself. TradingView sends each of the bot's orders to CrossTrade as a webhook. The webhook URL is the address those messages go to.
  • Contract: a futures contract for one month, for example Micro Nasdaq for December. Each has its own short code.
  • Root: the letters that name a futures product: MNQ for Micro Nasdaq, MGC for Micro Gold. Every contract code starts with its root.
  • Front month: the current contract: the nearest contract month that has not ended yet. It is the one your bot's orders have to name.
  • Position: the contracts your account holds right now.
  • Working order (also called a resting order): an order that is placed but has not filled yet, such as a stop or a target.
  • Flat: no position and no working orders. "The strategy is flat" means the bot holds no trade.
  • Demo account: a practice account with simulated money.
  • Prop firm: a company that gives you an evaluation or funded account to trade under its rules.
  • ETH: extended trading hours, the full trading day including the evening and night. RTH, regular trading hours, is the daytime part only.
  • New York time: US Eastern time. If you live somewhere else, search "time in New York" once and note the difference from your own clock. Times on this page use the 24-hour clock: 16:09 is 4:09 in the afternoon.
  • Invite-only: TradingView's name for a script that is shared privately with chosen usernames. Your bot reaches you this way.

How to work out your contract code

The chart uses a symbol that never expires (MNQ1!, MGC1!). An order has to name one dated contract. The code is the root, then a month letter, then the last digit of the year:

MNQZ6 (example)
MNQ · Z = December · 6 = 2026
MGCG7 (example)
MGC · G = February · 7 = 2027
  • Sign in to Tradovate's web app at trader.tradovate.com, with the login you linked in step 2.If your prop firm gave you its own address for the Tradovate platform, sign in there instead.
  • In the Search Bar at the top of the app, type the root: MNQ or MGC.
  • Click a result. A Quote Info window opens. Its Front Month line says whether this is the front-month contract, which is the current one. If it is not, click the next result and look again.
  • Type the code of the front-month contract into the contract box, written in exactly the format above.

One alert, and only one

  • A second account never gets an alert of its own. To run the bot on more accounts, use the second guide, One bot on several accounts. A second alert makes trades happen twice.
  • Never create a second alert for the same strategy on the same account. Both would send orders to it.
  • The alert remembers the settings it was created with. If you change a setting later, delete the alert and create a new one, while the strategy is flat (it holds no trade).
  • Editing or re-saving a running alert restarts it, which cancels that contract's working orders on the account. Only do it while the strategy is flat.
  • If you name the alert, never put your key or account name in the name.

After the setup

Running it day to day

What the bot does at the end of each day

Every trading day at 16:09 New York time the strategy closes any open position and cancels any resting order, on the chart and on your account. It places nothing new until the evening session opens at 18:00. You do not have to do anything.

That window, 16:09 to 18:00 New York time, is also the safe time for anything that restarts the alert: changing a setting, updating the contract code, deleting and re-creating the alert.

When the contract month changes

A futures contract ends, and trading moves on to the next contract month every few months (four times a year for MNQ). The code in your settings then points at the old contract.

  1. Now and then, look up the front-month contract again (how to work it out).
  2. When it has changed, wait until the bot holds no trade. Between 16:09 and 18:00 New York time is simplest.
  3. Type the new code in the contract box (step 5).
  4. Delete the alert and create it again (step 6).
  5. In Tradovate, check that nothing is left in the old contract.
Changing the size: the Multiplier

Multiplier (× the strategy's contracts), in the group ★ SIZING ★, multiplies every order the strategy places. At 1 the strategy uses its own sizing. At 2 every order doubles, and risk and profit both double. Drawdown grows by the same factor.

  • Slipstream: order size is Contracts per trade, in the same group, times the Multiplier.
  • Midas: each of its models has its own fixed contract count, and the Multiplier multiplies all of them. With the Multiplier at 1, the List of Trades in TradingView's Strategy Tester shows the contracts each order uses.

Choose the size by the drawdown your account can carry, not by the profit you hope for. Because the alert keeps its settings, a new Multiplier needs a new alert.

Holidays and early-close days
  • Market closed all day: nothing to do.
  • Early-close days (the exchange shuts before its usual time, for example around US Thanksgiving and Christmas): the two bots differ.
    • Slipstream has the exchange's 2026 early-close days written into it and closes ahead of them. It does not know early closes after 2026 or other exchange holidays. On such a day, a position open at the early close is carried to the reopen and closed there.
    • Midas does not know about early closes. A position still open at an early close is carried through the break and managed as usual after the reopen.
  • If you would rather sit a holiday out: after the 16:09 close on the last normal day, stop the alert in TradingView's alerts list. Restart it after 16:09 and before 18:00 New York time on the day normal trading resumes, or any time the strategy on the chart is flat.

If something is wrong

Troubleshooting

The alert stopped

An alert that is not Active sends nothing, and the bot is no longer managing your account. Check Tradovate first: if a position or working orders are still there, they are yours to manage by hand until the alert runs again.

  • It expired. Create it again (step 6), with Expiration set to Open-ended or to the furthest date your plan offers.
  • TradingView stopped it for firing too often. TradingView stops a script alert that fires more than 15 times within three minutes. Restart it from the alerts list. If it happens again, write to us.
  • The script hit an error: see "The script shows an error" below.

Restart or re-create an alert while the strategy on the chart is flat. TradingView's note on stopped alerts.

Nothing arrives in CrossTrade
  • Send live orders to CrossTrade (off = log only) is not ticked, or the key, the account or the contract box is empty. Fix the setting (step 5), then delete the alert and create it again (step 6).
  • The alert has no webhook URL, or two-factor authentication is off in TradingView. Open the alert log in TradingView: click the clock icon on the right edge, then the Log tab beside Alerts. Its Webhook status column shows delivery problems.
  • The alert's condition is not alert() function calls only.
  • The market is closed. With no new price bars, no messages are sent.
  • Your TradingView plan cannot send webhooks (the free Basic plan cannot).

TradingView's webhook instructions

CrossTrade shows red rows

A red row on a Cancel Order that says the order had already filled or finished is normal. On a red Place Order row, the message says what was wrong. The common causes:

  • The account name does not match. Copy it again from My Account, Brokers, and paste it into Tradovate account in the strategy's settings.
  • The Tradovate link expired. Click Link Demo or Link Live again and sign back in. Messages sent while the link was down are not queued.
  • The Kill Switch is on in CrossTrade's Master Controls. It is CrossTrade's own stop switch: while it is on, CrossTrade refuses every alert. Switch it off there.
  • The key is wrong, for example it was cut short when pasted.
  • The contract code is wrong or expired. See the next item.

After fixing a strategy setting, delete the alert and create it again.

Wrong contract month, or the contract expired

Signs: red rows in Alert History that name the instrument, or orders landing in a contract month you did not mean to trade.

  1. Wait until the strategy is flat. After 16:09 New York time is simplest.
  2. Open the strategy's settings and type the current code in the contract box (how to work it out).
  3. Delete the old alert and create a new one (step 6).
  4. In Tradovate, check that nothing is left in the old contract.
The script shows an error: wrong timeframe or symbol

Slipstream runs only on MNQ and Midas only on MGC, each on the 1-minute chart. Anywhere else the script stops with a message. Put the chart back on the right symbol and 1 minute (the chart links are in step 4).

An alert keeps the symbol and timeframe of the chart it was created on. An alert created on the wrong chart has to be deleted and created again on the right one.

I changed a setting and nothing changed

A TradingView alert keeps the strategy settings from the moment it was created. Delete the alert and create a new one for the change to take effect, while the strategy is flat.

The account does not match the chart

The usual cause is a trade or an order placed by hand in the same contract on the same account, or a second alert on the same account. Stop the alert, close that contract's position and orders in Tradovate, remove any duplicate alert, and start again when the strategy on the chart is flat. Keep trading by hand on a different account.

I think my key was exposed

Stop the alert first, then check Tradovate for positions and orders you did not expect. In CrossTrade open My Account, Security, and click Regenerate Key. Put the new key into the strategy's settings (step 5) and create a new alert (step 6). Do not send the old or the new key to anyone, us included.

Still stuck? Write to support@allfluencetrading.com or ask in the Discord. Say which bot, which step and what the screen shows. Cover your key, webhook URL, account name and the text after the alert's name in any screenshot.
